# Chapter 2 — A Nation Is Born

_Monday, 9 August 1965. Queenstown, Singapore._

> **Status:** implemented on branch `ch2-independence` (25 Sep 2026; in-game text: `src/chapters/ind-text.js`,
> which is the source of truth where the two differ). Facts and quotes checked against
> [docs/research/1965-history.md](research/1965-history.md). Lines marked **[native review]** need a
> native speaker to check them before they ship.

**Voices in 1965** (people speak in short spoken sentences and react to each other; each line is ≤ 120 characters):
- **Boon (30):** runs the kopitiam. Loud and quick like Ah Ma, even says her "Aiyoh". Jokes to hide
  that he's scared. He hasn't trusted big news since his papa disappeared.
- **Siti (35):** a teacher on the afternoon session. She still reads everything, but she's careful now.
  She hasn't forgotten the 1942 headline.
- **Farid (16):** Siti's little brother; afternoon-session student who helps at the counter in the mornings. Cheeky and
  full of questions. He wants the day to be exciting.
- **Mr. Rajan (63):** once the ARP warden, now a grassroots leader at the community centre. Calm, dry, short sentences.
- **Ravi (16):** Mr. Rajan's son. Quiet, reads a comic, watches everything. Almost never speaks.
- **Ah Pek Tan:** old Hokkien uncle, speaks mostly Hokkien, a regular for thirty years. Grumbles.
- **Makcik Rohani:** Malay auntie, a seamstress on the second floor. Warm, worried, sharp.
- **Auntie Letchumi:** elderly Tamil auntie, speaks mostly Tamil. Polite and proud.

**Kopi orders.** Each order is built in three taps: *drink* · *milk* · *sugar* (+ *peng* for iced).
A chalk order board at the counter teaches the words:

| Word | Means | From |
|---|---|---|
| kopi / teh | coffee / tea (with condensed milk and sugar, unless you say otherwise) | Malay / Hokkien |
| -O | no milk | Hokkien 烏 *o* (black) |
| -C | evaporated milk | Hainanese/Hokkien (see research) |
| kosong | no sugar | Malay (zero) |
| siew dai | less sweet | Hokkien/Cantonese |
| ga dai | more sweet | Hokkien/Cantonese |
| peng | iced | Hokkien 冰 |

A wrong drink never fails you. The customer just laughs and corrects you, and you remake it.

## 2 · Morning rush — 9:30 a.m.

> **[Title card]** Queenstown, Singapore. Monday, 9 August 1965.

*(The Rediffusion box on the wall murmurs a Hokkien storyteller. The radio on the counter plays a Malay song on Radio Singapura; Makcik Rohani asked for it. Sparky appears in the kopitiam doorway. Boon, behind the counter, does a double take.)*

**BOON (30):** Eh… Sparky? No. Can’t be.

**BOON (30):** Twenty-three years, and you haven’t changed one bit! Ah Ma always said the bear would come back.

**BOON (30):** Come, come. I said I’d make kopi better than Ah Ma’s, right? Now you can help me prove it.

*(Sparky nods eagerly)*

**FARID (16):** Abang [big brother] Boon, who’s this?

**BOON (30):** Old friend. Farid, show him the board. Sparky, two orders waiting. Go!

*Objective: Make the drinks. Check the order board if you forget.*

*Order 1 — Ah Pek Tan (Hokkien):*

**AH PEK TAN:** *Kopi-O kosong!* [black coffee, no sugar] Like always. Thirty years, same order!

→ correct: **AH PEK TAN:** Hmm. Not bad. Not as good as your Ah Ma’s. Not bad.

→ wrong: **AH PEK TAN:** Aiyoh, got milk inside! O means black lah, bear. Again.

*Order 2 — Makcik Rohani (Malay):*

**MAKCIK ROHANI:** *Teh-C,* [tea with evaporated milk] *kurang manis* [less sweet], ya?

**FARID (16):** *Kurang manis* is Malay. Here we say *siew dai.* Same thing, different word!

→ correct: **MAKCIK ROHANI:** *Terima kasih* [thank you], adik. You learn faster than Boon did.

→ wrong: **MAKCIK ROHANI:** So sweet! You want my teeth to fall out?

*Optional chats (morning):*

- **AH PEK TAN**
  - Indonesia still sending bombs. Remember MacDonald House? In the middle of Orchard Road!
  - Last time one cup kopi so cheap. Now everything also go up. Aiyoh.
- **MAKCIK ROHANI**
  - My husband says KL and Singapore are always quarrelling now. Like a married couple, he says.
  - Last year, during the riots, I didn’t go downstairs for a week. I can still hear the shouting.
- **AUNTIE LETCHUMI**
  - *Vanakkam* [hello]. **[native review]** *(pats the stool beside her; nobody sits there)*
- **FARID (16)**
  - Kak [big sister] Siti taught me to read before I could even walk properly. She never lets me forget.
  - Afternoon session, so mornings I work here. Abang Boon pays me in kaya toast.
- **RAVI (16)** *(reading a comic at Mr. Rajan’s empty table)*
  - …Hm? Oh. My father’s at the community centre. He said wait here.

*Notice (Look closer): customers sit in separate groups. Hokkien uncles by the counter, Malay aunties by the window, Auntie Letchumi alone near the door.*

**Look closer** (camera hotspots):

- *The Rediffusion box:* A wooden box on the wall. It plays stories and songs all day. — photo card: **Rediffusion** — Wired radio, from 1949. For $5 a month a cable brought music and famous Chinese dialect storytellers into homes and coffee shops.
- *The television:* Boon’s pride and joy. Most families don’t own one yet. — photo card: **Television Singapura** — Singapore’s TV service began on 15 February 1963. Only about 1 in 12 homes had a set, so people gathered at community centres and coffee shops to watch.
- *The money tin:* The coins in Boon’s money tin. — photo card: **Whose dollar?** — In 1965 Singapore still used the Malaya and British Borneo dollar. Its own money came in June 1967. The new $1 note showed Queenstown flats!

## 3 · Ten o’clock

*(The Malay song on the counter radio stops mid-verse. A pause. Then an announcer reads, in Malay.)*

**RADIO (Radio Singapura, Malay service):** *(the broadcast went out in Malay; in the game the player reads the lines, marked “(in Malay)”, with the Proclamation’s key sentence in English. The surviving Malay recording is held by the National Archives and is not in the game.)*

> On-screen, the key sentence of the Proclamation, in English:
> “…Singapore shall be forever a sovereign democratic and independent nation, founded upon the principles of liberty and justice…”

*(Makcik Rohani’s cup stops halfway to her mouth. She understands first. Everyone else watches her face.)*

**MAKCIK ROHANI:** Singapura… keluar dari Malaysia? [Singapore… out of Malaysia?] **[native review]**

**FARID (16):** *(to Boon, in English)* We’re out of Malaysia. He said independent. That means we’re our own country?

**AH PEK TAN:** Ah? What he say? *To̍k-li̍p* [independent]? Who independent?

*(Ah Pek Tan’s spoon clinks against his cup. Nobody moves.)*

**BOON (30):** *(very quietly)* Every time the news changes, somebody disappears.

**BOON (30):** Sparky. Go get Siti. She’s down the corridor at the bookshop. She’ll know what it means.

## 4 · Down the corridor

*Objective: Find Siti at the bookshop.*

*Ambient rumours as Sparky passes (overheard, no need to stop):*

- **BARBER:** No more water from tomorrow! Johor will turn off the pipe, you wait!
- **SCHOOLBOY:** My brother says the army from KL is coming to take over!
- **HAWKER:** Independent? Then who’s going to buy my noodles? The British?
- **GIRL:** Does it mean no school tomorrow?
- **SCHOOLBOY 2:** My cousin says they’re letting off firecrackers in Chinatown! Firecrackers, for what?

*(Siti is at the bookshop with a stack of exercise books and a morning paper.)*

**SITI (35):** Sparky? *(she stares, then laughs)* You look exactly the same. Exactly!

**SITI (35):** You heard? This morning’s paper doesn’t say a single word about it. First time the paper’s behind.

**SITI (35):** Boon wants me to explain it to everyone? Sparky… no.

**SITI (35):** Last time I read the news out, I told the whole street we’d be safe. Remember?

*Heirloom — if Sparky saved Siti’s newspaper in 1942:*

**SITI (35):** *(takes a folded, yellowed front page from her book bag)* I still carry this. “It SHALL stand.”

**SITI (35):** It reminds me to read carefully. And never promise what I don’t know.

*Fallback:*

**SITI (35):** I think about that headline every time I open a newspaper.

*(Sparky tugs her sleeve towards the kopitiam)*

**SITI (35):** Okay. Okay! No promises. I’ll just tell them the truth.

## 5 · Worries rush

*(Back in the kopitiam. The room is louder now, but nobody’s drinking.)*

**BOON (30):** Siti! Good. Sparky, keep the drinks coming. Everyone’s asking. Nobody’s ordering properly.

*Objective: Take each order. Bring Siti’s answer with the drink.*

*Each order: the customer orders and asks a worried question. Sparky makes the drink, walks to Siti’s corner table, she answers, and Sparky carries the drink and the answer back.*

*Worry 1 — Ah Pek Tan (water):*

**AH PEK TAN:** *Kopi peng* [iced coffee]! More ice. Drink ice now, tomorrow maybe no water!

**SITI (35):** Tell him: the water agreements with Johor stay. It’s written into the separation deal.

→ **AH PEK TAN:** Written down, ah? Hm. Okay. Ice still very nice.

*Fact card — **Water from across the Causeway**: Much of Singapore’s water came from Johor. The Separation Agreement promised that the 1961 and 1962 water deals would carry on. Even so, Singapore spent decades learning to make more of its own water.*

*Worry 2 — Makcik Rohani (the riots):*

**MAKCIK ROHANI:** *Teh-C siew dai.* *(she says the Hokkien words carefully)* Ask Siti: will the riots come back?

**SITI (35):** I don’t know. I won’t pretend I do. Last year I was scared of people I’d known all my life.

**SITI (35):** But look who’s making her tea. A bear, for a Malay makcik, in a Chinese kopitiam. Tell her that.

→ **MAKCIK ROHANI:** *(looks at Ah Pek, then at Sparky, then laughs a little)* A bear. Okay. A bear I can trust.

*Fact card — **The 1964 riots**: In July and September 1964, fights broke out between Chinese and Malay people in Singapore. 36 people were killed and hundreds were hurt. Families had to stay indoors during curfews. A year later, many still feared it could happen again.*

*Worry 3 — Auntie Letchumi (jobs):*

**AUNTIE LETCHUMI:** *Teh-O* **[native review: short Tamil line asking “what will happen to the naval base jobs?”]**

**RAVI (16):** *(looks up from his comic)* She’s asking about the naval base. Her son works there. Is his job gone?

**SITI (35):** The British bases are still here. Nothing closes today. Tell her: nothing closes today.

**RAVI (16):** *(in Tamil to Auntie Letchumi)* **[native review]**

→ **AUNTIE LETCHUMI:** *Nandri* [thank you]. **[native review]** *(she pats the stool beside her again; this time Makcik Rohani sits)*

*Fact card — **The British bases**: Thousands of Singaporeans worked at the British military bases. The bases stayed after 1965. In 1968 Britain announced it would leave, and its forces were gone by the end of 1971.*

*(Visual payoff: over the rush, chairs turn. By the last worry, the tables have been dragged into one long row.)*

**SITI (35):** *(to Sparky, quietly)* That wasn’t so bad. No promises. Just the truth.

## 6 · The evening crowd

> **[Title card]** Later that day.

*(The whole block crowds in for the TV. People fill the doorway and stand on stools. Mr. Rajan arrives from the community centre and sits with Ravi.)*

**MR. RAJAN:** Evening, Boon. Evening… Sparky? *(beat)* Of course it’s Sparky. Why not. Today anything can happen.

*Heirloom — if Sparky saved the rice in 1942:*

**MR. RAJAN:** Boon. Your Ah Ma’s rice kept us alive in that shelter. I never paid her back. Tonight’s kopi is on me.

*Fallback:* **MR. RAJAN:** Busy night, Boon. Your Ah Ma would be proud.

*Heirloom — if Sparky saved the family photo:* *(Papa’s photo hangs on the wall beside Ah Ma’s portrait.)* **BOON (30):** Papa always gets the best seat. Right above the TV.

*Kindness payoff — if Sparky helped tie the bundles in 1942:*

**NEIGHBOUR (now 50s):** You’re the bear who held my rope! In ’42! My hands were shaking so badly. Remember?

*Kindness payoff — if Sparky brought Pak Hassan water:*

**SITI (35):** Pak Hassan used to talk about you. “That bear brought me water, better than satay.” He’d have loved tonight.

*One last order — the cross-order:*

**AH PEK TAN:** Bear! *Teh-C* for the Tamil auntie. *Siew dai.* I asked her already. *(waves at Auntie Letchumi)*

**AUNTIE LETCHUMI:** *(nods, smiling)* Siew dai!

→ **BOON (30):** Wah. Now even Ah Pek is taking orders. Maybe I should pay him in kaya toast too.

*(Farid squeezes onto the bench. The only space left is beside Ravi. They nod at each other and don’t speak.)*

## 7 · The press conference

*(Boon turns up the TV. Real footage: Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew’s press conference, recorded at noon that day at Broadcasting House, Caldecott Hill, and shown on television the same day. Subtitles are verbatim from the National Archives transcript, with pauses shown as “…”.)*

**LEE KUAN YEW (footage, clip 1):** “For me, it is a moment of anguish because all my life… the whole of my adult life…
I have believed in Merger and the unity of these two territories.”

**LEE KUAN YEW (footage):** “Would you mind if we stop for a while.”

*(The recording stops so he can recover. Cut to black for a moment. In the kopitiam nobody moves. Boon has his hand over his mouth.)*

**FARID (16):** *(whispering)* He’s crying. The Prime Minister is crying.

**SITI (35):** *(quietly)* He really didn’t want this.

**LEE KUAN YEW (footage, clip 2):** “We are going to have a multi-racial nation in Singapore. We will set the example.”

**LEE KUAN YEW (footage):** “This is not a Malay nation; this is not a Chinese nation; this is not an Indian nation.”

**LEE KUAN YEW (footage):** “Everybody will have his place: equal; language, culture, religion.”

*(Clip timecodes to pick from the NAS recording, 1:03:28 long. Keep the whole TV beat under ~30 s.)*

*(The camera drifts across the room: Ah Pek Tan, Makcik Rohani, Auntie Letchumi on the shared stool, Siti, Mr. Rajan, Ravi and Farid on the bench, Boon behind the counter. Nobody drinks.)*

**BOON (30):** Today needs to go on the wall, Sparky. You choose.

## Album & credits (chapter 2)

**Real vs imagined:**
- Imagined: Boon’s kopitiam, Siti, Farid, Ravi, Mr. Rajan, Ah Pek Tan, Makcik Rohani and Auntie Letchumi.
- Real: on 9 August 1965 Singapore separated from Malaysia. The Proclamation went out on Radio Singapura at 10 a.m. The Malay reading you hear is the only 1965 recording known to survive.
- Real: Lee Kuan Yew’s press conference, recorded at noon and shown on TV that day, including his tears and his words about a multiracial nation. The footage and words are real.
- Real: some people let off firecrackers in Chinatown that morning. Others were shocked and afraid. Reactions were mixed.
- Real: *The Straits Times* on 10 August 1965 ran the headline “Singapore is out”.
- Real: the worries in the kopitiam: water from Johor, the 1964 riots, jobs at the British bases, Indonesia’s Confrontation.
- Real: kopitiam lingo mixes Hokkien, Malay and English, and people of every community ordered in it.
- Real: Singapore had just two infantry battalions in 1965. Many of its soldiers were on their way to Sabah that week.
- Imagined: the rumours in the corridor stand for the many that spread that day.
