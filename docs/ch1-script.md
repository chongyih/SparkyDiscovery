# Chapter 1 — The Fortress Falls

_12–15 February 1942 (epilogue: March 1942). Content note: This chapter shows wartime air raids, fear and loss. Nothing graphic is shown._

## 0 · Prologue — a Queenstown void deck, today

**MR. BOON (91):** Ah, you found my old Brownie camera. Careful, ah. It’s even older than me. And I’m ninety-one!

**MR. BOON (91):** There are still three photos left on this roll. I never took them. Three days I could never forget.

**MR. BOON (91):** The first one is 1942. I was seven years old, hiding under a table in my Ah Ma [grandma]’s kopitiam [coffee shop].

**MR. BOON (91):** Go on. Look through it. Maybe you’ll see what I saw.

*(Sparky peers into the viewfinder)*

## 1 · Then & Now — Telok Ayer today

> **[Title card]** Telok Ayer, Chinatown. Today.

**MR. BOON (91):** It’s all cafés and tourists now. But back then, this street was my whole world.

*Objective: Raise the camera. Line up the old photo with the street.*

**MR. BOON (91):** There. That’s it. That’s exactly how it looked.

## 2 · Morning papers — Thursday, 12 February 1942

> **[Title card]** Chinatown, Singapore. Thursday, 12 February 1942.

**NARRATOR:** Four days ago, Japanese soldiers crossed over from Johor and landed on Singapore island.

**NARRATOR:** The newspapers still say “Fortress Singapore”. But look at the sky. That isn’t rain cloud. It’s smoke.

**SITI (12):** Eh, you! Little bear! You look lost. Want to help me? My bapak [dad]’s paper stand is crazy busy today.

*(Sparky nods eagerly)*

**SITI (12):** Bagus [Great]! Take these two. One Chinese paper for the medical hall, one Malay paper for the provision shop.

**SITI (12):** Front page says “Singapore will stand!” I read it out to the whole street this morning.

**SITI (12):** But if we’re so safe… why is everyone packing?

*Objective: Deliver the two newspapers.*

*Deliver to the medical hall (the Chinese newspaper):*

**SHOPKEEPER:** Ah, the Nanyang Siang Pau [Chinese newspaper]! Thank you. Might be the last one for a while.

*Fact card — Nanyang Siang Pau — “South Seas Business News”: One of Singapore’s big Chinese newspapers. Like the others, it stopped printing just before Singapore fell, and only came back in September 1945.*

*Deliver to the provision shop (the Malay newspaper):*

**SHOPKEEPER:** Utusan Melayu [Malay newspaper]! Good. The rice sacks are almost empty, but at least the news still comes.

*Fact card — Utusan Melayu — “The Malay Messenger”: The first Malay newspaper owned and run by Malays, started here in 1939. One of its founders, Yusof Ishak, later became Singapore’s first President.*

**Optional chats** (walk up to anyone):

- **SITI (12)**
  - People say the Japanese are already at Bukit Timah. Funny, the paper doesn’t say that.
  - {Mak|Mum} says one day I might get a little brother. I’ll teach him to read. First thing!
  - Every morning I read the headlines out loud. Somebody has to tell the street what’s going on.
- **MR. RAJAN**
  - Morning, little bear. See the tape on the windows? If a bomb falls, the glass won’t fly everywhere.
  - I’m the {ARP|Air Raid Precautions} warden here. No pay. Just this helmet and a whistle. But it’s my street.
  - Lots of rumours today. Don’t believe everything you hear. Stay calm. Stay near the shelter.
- **AH MA**
  - Rice, salt, candles. Buy them today. Tomorrow? Who knows.
  - Aiyoh, Boon! Come out from under that table and wipe the cups!
  - The tap water is getting weaker every day. Fill every pot you can find, bear.
- **AH BOON (7)**
  - Is that a real camera? Wah! One day I’m going to have one just like it.
  - When I grow up, I’m going to make kopi even better than Ah Ma’s. Shh, don’t tell her.
  - My papa’s helping at the docks. He promised he’ll be home for Chinese New Year.
- **PAK HASSAN**
  - Hear that? Boom… boom. Big guns, near Bukit Timah. Louder than yesterday.
  - No satay today, {adik|little one}. My ankle’s useless! So Siti brings me the news instead. Clever girl, that one.
  - The family next door left last night, everything on a cart. But go where? We’re on an island.

**Optional kindnesses** (remembered in later chapters):

- *Help tie the bundles* — NEIGHBOUR: “Adik, can you hold this rope? My hands are shaking too much to tie it.” → “Thank you. We’re going to my sister’s in Geylang. At least we’ll be together.”
- *Bring Pak Hassan some water* — PAK HASSAN: “Adik, the tap by the kopitiam still works. Could you bring an old man a cup? My ankle, you see.” → “Ahh. Better than satay. Well… almost. Thank you, adik.”

**Look closer** (camera hotspots):

- *Poster:* A war poster: “Fortress Singapore”. Behind it, black smoke is rising. — photo card: **“Fortress Singapore”** — People called Singapore the “Gibraltar of the East”. Its big coastal guns could turn to face land, but they mostly had shells made for sinking ships, not stopping soldiers.
- *Bicycle:* An ordinary bicycle. Japanese soldiers rode bikes just like this all the way down Malaya. — photo card: **The bicycle blitzkrieg** — Japanese soldiers rode bicycles, many taken from locals, all the way down Malaya. They moved faster than the British could retreat. Malaya fell in 55 days; the Causeway was blown up on 31 January 1942.
- *Smoke:* Black smoke from burning oil tanks, miles away. It has hung over the city for days. — photo card: **A sky of black smoke** — As the Japanese got closer, oil tanks were set on fire so the enemy couldn’t use the fuel. Thick black smoke hung over the city for days.

**SITI (12):** Done already? Terima kasih [Thank you]! You’re even faster than my—

*(Sparky jumps at the siren)*

## 3 · Air raid

**MR. RAJAN:** Air raid! Air raid! Everybody off the street, now!

**MR. RAJAN:** Bear! Ah Ma, Ah Boon and Pak Hassan are still out here. Get them to the shelter. Quick!

**MR. RAJAN:** Stay close to the walls. And don’t stop to look up!

*Objective: Find Ah Ma, Ah Boon and Pak Hassan. Bring them to the shelter.*

*Finding Ah Ma:*

**AH MA:** My shop… my money tin… Aiyoh, forget it! Where’s Boon? Have you seen Boon?

**AH MA:** Okay, okay. You lead, bear. I’m right behind you.

*Finding Ah Boon (under a kopitiam table):*

**AH BOON (7):** No! I’m not coming out! The planes are too loud!

**AH BOON (7):** I want Papa! Papa’s at the docks!

**AH MA:** *(if Ah Ma is with you)* Boon. Look at me. Papa knows how to take cover. Now hold my hand. Tight.

**AH BOON (7):** Okay… but you go first, bear.

*Finding Pak Hassan (by his broken satay cart):*

**PAK HASSAN:** Go on without me, adik. I’m too slow—

**PAK HASSAN:** What, you won’t leave me? Aiyah [Oh dear]. Okay, okay. Slowly, ya?

*While they follow you (random): AH BOON (7): “Don’t let go! Don’t let go!” · AH MA: “Don’t look up, Boon. Just watch the bear.” · PAK HASSAN: “Keep going! I’m still here!” · MR. RAJAN: “This way! Past the lamp post! Keep coming!”*

*A shophouse on the route is hit:*

**MR. RAJAN:** Get down!

**AH BOON (7):** That’s Uncle Lim’s shop! Is he inside? Ah Ma, is he inside?

**MR. RAJAN:** The fire team’s coming. We can’t help him by standing here. Move!

**AH MA:** Don’t look back, Boon. Don’t look back.

*At the shelter:*

**MR. RAJAN:** In, in, in! Heads down. One, two, three… and the bear. That’s everyone.

**MR. RAJAN:** Good job, bear. Now we wait.

## 4 · Room for two things

**MR. RAJAN:** No bags! There’s no room in there. Two things per family, that’s all.

**AH MA:** Two? Aiyoh. Bear, help me choose. Quickly!

*Choice: There’s room for two things. What will you bring into the shelter?*

- **The family photo** → AH MA: “Boon’s papa is in that photo. Good. We keep him close.”
- **The sack of rice** → AH MA: “Rice. Smart bear. Nobody can be brave on an empty stomach.”
- **Siti’s newspaper** → SITI (12): “You’re keeping it? Then I’ll read it to everyone in there.”
- **Papa’s tiffin carrier (lunch tins)** → AH BOON (7): “That’s Papa’s {tiffin carrier|stacked lunch tins}! He’ll need it when he comes back.”

*Inside the shelter:*

**MR. RAJAN:** Everybody down! Backs to the wall, hands over your heads!

**AH BOON (7):** Ah Ma, the walls are shaking! Make it stop!

**PAK HASSAN:** The candle! Somebody hold on to the matches—

*(The candle goes out.)*

> **[Title card]** Two nights later. Saturday, 14 February 1942.

**AH MA:** Boon? Boon! His mat is empty. Where’s my Boon?

**SITI (12):** He kept asking about his papa. He said the fire post is just on Neil Road…

**MR. RAJAN:** He went out there? In this?

**MR. RAJAN:** I can’t leave everyone here. Bear, you’re small and fast. Find him. Stay under the five-foot ways.

## 5 · Blackout search — night of 14 February

**NARRATOR:** Blackout. Every light in the city is off. Except the fires.

*Objective: Follow Boon’s trail. Stay under the five-foot ways. — Tip: Hear a whistle? A shell is coming. Get under cover, fast!*

*If caught in the open: Too close! Your ears are ringing. Get back under cover. / The blast knocks you flat. Up you get. Stay covered this time. / Dust everywhere. Find a five-foot way!*

*Clue 1 — A wooden clog: One of Boon’s clogs, in the middle of the road. He didn’t even stop to pick it up.*

*Clue 2 — Papa’s tiffin carrier: Papa’s tiffin carrier, still warm. Boon was bringing his papa dinner.*

*Clue 3 — A small voice: Someone is calling, somewhere ahead…*

**AH BOON (7):** (far away) Papa? Papa, where are you?

*Finding Boon (behind a tipped handcart):*

**AH BOON (7):** Bear? Bear! You came!

**AH BOON (7):** Papa wasn’t at the fire post. They said he went to another fire. But everything is on fire!

**AH BOON (7):** I brought his photo. So I could ask people if they’ve seen him.

*(Sparky gives a hug)*

**AH BOON (7):** Can we go back now? Hold my hand. Don’t let go, okay?

*Back at the shelter:*

**AH MA:** Boon! You silly, silly boy. Never do that again, you hear me? Never.

**AH MA:** Thank you, bear. Come. Both of you. Sit close to me.

## 6 · Rumours in the silence — 15 February

> **[Title card]** Sunday, 15 February 1942. The first day of Chinese New Year.

**PAK HASSAN:** Listen. The guns… they’ve stopped.

**SITI (12):** Is it over? Did we win?

**AH MA:** Nobody knows anything. Go and ask around, bear. And grab that newspaper. It’s blowing everywhere.

*Objective: Ask the neighbours what they’ve heard. Collect the torn newspaper.*

**PAK HASSAN:** Someone said American ships are coming to save us. Maybe that’s why it’s so quiet?

**PAK HASSAN:** Here. A bit of this morning’s paper. It blew right onto my foot.

**SOLDIER:** I saw our officers driving up Bukit Timah Road. One of them was holding a white flag.

**SOLDIER:** Take this. I… I don’t even know where my unit is any more.

**SITI (12):** The goldsmith’s radio picked up a faraway station. It said the fighting is still going on. Hours ago.

**SITI (12):** I found this bit by his door. I think it’s the headline!

*Puzzle — The last newspaper: the torn front page reads “Singapore Must Stand; It SHALL Stand” (The Straits Times, 15 February 1942. A message from the Governor, Sir Shenton Thomas (NewspaperSG).)*

**SITI (12):** “Singapore must stand. It SHALL stand.” That’s what it says. This morning’s paper.

**MR. RAJAN:** Siti. Put the paper down. I’ve just come from the police post.

**MR. RAJAN:** The British have surrendered. This evening, at the Ford Factory in Bukit Timah. That’s why the guns stopped.

**PAK HASSAN:** Surrendered? The great fortress? Just like that?

**SITI (12):** I read that headline to everyone. I told them we’d be okay.

**AH BOON (7):** Ah Ma, the bombs stopped. That’s good, right?

**AH MA:** Quiet doesn’t always mean safe, Boon.

**MR. RAJAN:** I don’t know what happens next. But we face it together. Nobody on this street is alone.

*Photo card: **“It SHALL Stand”** — That morning, The Straits Times printed the Governor’s message: “Singapore Must Stand; It SHALL Stand.” That evening, the British surrendered. Most people heard the news by word of mouth.*

## 7 · Syonan-to — March 1942

> **[Title card]** Syonan-to. March 1942.

**SITI (12):** They even changed our name. We’re not Singapore any more. Now it’s “Syonan-to [Light of the South Island]”.

**PAK HASSAN:** See the sentry at the corner? Always bow when you pass. People who forget get slapped. Or worse.

**SITI (12):** Bapak’s papers are banned. Only the Japanese newspaper is allowed now.

**MR. RAJAN:** No more armband for me. Now I queue for rice like everyone else. Head down, mouth shut.

**AH MA:** After the surrender, all the Chinese men had to go to Hong Lim Green [a park nearby] to be “screened”.

**AH MA:** Boon’s papa went, like they told him. That was four weeks ago.

**AH MA:** Every morning I pour his kopi [coffee]. Every night, I pour it away.

**AH BOON (7):** Ah Ma says Papa’s coming back. He is, right, bear?

*(Sparky gives a hug)*

**AH MA:** Here, little bear. “Banana money”. Their money. Every week it buys less and less rice.

**AH MA:** Keep it. One day this will be just paper. I hope that day comes soon.

*Photo card: **“Banana money”** — The Japanese printed their own money. The $10 note had banana trees on it. They printed so much that prices shot up. By 1945, the notes were worthless.*

**NARRATOR:** The Japanese Occupation lasted three years and seven months. People went hungry and lived in fear. In the Sook Ching, thousands of Chinese men were taken away and killed. Many families, like Boon’s, never found out what happened. On 12 September 1945, Japan formally surrendered in Singapore. The people who lived through it never forgot, so that we would remember too.

## Back at the void deck

**MR. BOON (91):** Papa never came home. Ah Ma kept the kopitiam going, and I grew up behind that counter.

**MR. BOON (91):** That’s why I kept this camera, little bear. Somebody has to remember.
