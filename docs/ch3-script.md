# Chapter 3 — The First Intake

_September 1967 to Saturday, 2 March 1968. Queenstown and Taman Jurong Camp, Singapore._

> **Status:** first draft (25 Sep 2026). Facts checked against
> [docs/research/1967-history.md](research/1967-history.md). The plan is in [design.md](design.md), in the Chapter 3 section.
> Lines marked **[native review]** need a native speaker to check them before they ship. The Malay drill
> commands also need an SAF or native reviewer, because no 1967 source confirms the exact wording.

**Voices in 1967–68** (people speak in short spoken sentences and react to each other; each line is ≤ 120 characters):
- **Boon (32):** still runs the kopitiam. Jokes less than in 1965. Farid is like a little brother to him,
  and he's scared, so he's cross. He won't open letters: in his experience they bring bad news.
- **Siti (37):** teacher. Farid's big sister. Calm and dry. She reads the news for everyone, as always.
- **Farid (18):** grown up since 1965. He wants to be the "somebody" Mr. Rajan talked about. Bossy when
  nervous. Calls Boon "Abang".
- **Ah Hock (18):** a hawker's son. Speaks Hokkien only. Big, stubborn, homesick. He says very little, and
  what he says is short.
- **Ravi (18):** Mr. Rajan's son. Still nearly silent. He watches, counts and remembers.
- **Leo Pereira (18):** Eurasian, English-educated, talks nonstop, and has seen every war film at the Capitol.
- **Sergeant Osman:** a Malay regular from before NS. Deadpan. Shouts drill in Malay, explains in English.
  Every joke is one sentence long. Secretly kind.
- **Mr. Rajan (~57):** grassroots leader. Ravi's father. Calm, dry, short sentences.

**Drill words.** Each command comes in two parts: a long *cautionary* word, then a sharp *executive* word.
You move on the executive word only. The album's "Drill words" card teaches them, like Chapter 2's kopi board:

| Command | Means | Input |
|---|---|---|
| *Sedia!* | Attention! | Space / tap |
| *Senang diri!* | Stand at ease! | (Sergeant only; no input) |
| *Ke kanan… pusing!* | Right… turn! | → |
| *Ke kiri… pusing!* | Left… turn! | ← |
| *Ke belakang… pusing!* | About… turn! | ↓ |
| *Dari kiri, cepat… jalan!* | From the left, quick… march! | ↑ |
| *Berhenti!* | Halt! | Space / tap |
| *Pandang… kanan!* | Eyes… right! | look right (mouse / drag) |
| *Pandang… depan!* | Eyes… front! | look ahead |
| *Sandang… senjata!* | Sling… arms! | ↑ |

**[SAF/native review]** for every form above. Research notes that the right turn is usually "ke kanan pusing" today.

Every command shows its icon and meaning. The right button counts from just before the word until well after it. A mistake never
fails you: on day one the sergeant says "Again!", and after two tries he lets it go ("Close enough."). At the parade, Ah Hock nudges
you back into line. There is no voice or cue sound: the words are on screen, and the boots answer.

## 0 · Back at the void deck

> **[Title card]** Queenstown, Singapore. Today.

**MR. BOON (91):** Farid was eighteen. One of the first boys ever called up to be a soldier. The very first batch.

**MR. BOON (91):** And I told him not to go. I shouted at him, even.

**MR. BOON (91):** Go on, Sparky. Last one. Look through it.

*(Sparky raises the camera)*

**MR. BOON (91):** But the field is the same field. That's where they marched.

*Objective: Raise the camera. Line up the old view with the field.*

hurry. A heritage marker was put up here in 2017.

## 2 · Send-off morning — September 1967

> **[Title card]** Queenstown, Singapore. September 1967.

*(The kopitiam, early. Chairs still on the tables. Farid's bag is packed by the door. Boon is wiping a clean counter,
very hard. Sparky waves from the doorway; Farid waves back and Boon nods. Sparky walks in down the aisle between the tables
and stops at the counter beside Farid.)*

**BOON (32):** Sparky? Two years, and here you are again. You only turn up when something big happens.

**FARID (18):** Abang, stop wiping. It's clean already.

**FARID (18):** I signed the papers at Kallang last month. It's done. Nine hundred of us, full-time. The first ever.

**BOON (32):** *(not looking up)* Good iron isn't made into nails. Good men don't become soldiers. Everybody knows that.

**FARID (18):** Nobody says that anymore.

**BOON (32):** I say it. So don't go.

*(Pause.)*

**FARID (18):** Mr. Rajan said somebody has to defend this place. I want it to be somebody like us.

**FARID (18):** Sparky's coming too. Right, Sparky?

*(Sparky salutes. Badly.)*

**BOON (32):** Aiyoh. Not you also.

**BOON (32):** *(turning away)* The kopitiam can't close. I'm not coming to the community centre. Go.

*(Boon goes into the back. When Farid picks up his bag, there's something on the counter that wasn't there before.)*

*Heirloom — if Sparky saved Papa's tiffin carrier in 1942:* *(Papa's old tiffin carrier, the stacked lunch tins, full and
still warm.)* **FARID (18):** This is… his papa's. He never lets anyone touch it.

*Fallback:* *(A new tiffin carrier, full and still warm.)* **FARID (18):** He packed lunch. He's angry, and he packed lunch.

*Ch2 wall payoff (one line, by the Chapter 2 choice):*
- **Newspaper** → **SITI (37):** *(arriving, reading Farid's call-up letter aloud)* "…to report for full-time National Service." Well. This time the news is true.
- **The state flag** → **FARID (18):** *(touching it on the way out)* This time I know the song. All the words.
- **The four-language sign** → *(Farid's name has been painted onto the bottom of the sign, small, in Boon's handwriting.)* **SITI (37):** He did that last night. Don't tell him you saw.
- **The calendar page** → *(Boon has pinned a second page beside 9 August 1965: today's date.)* **SITI (37):** He tore off today. So he'll remember.

**Look closer** (camera hotspots):

- *The money tin:* New notes in Boon's money tin. — photo card: **Our own dollar** — Singapore's own money came out on 12 June 1967. The new $1 note showed Queenstown's flats. Boon's customers pay with a picture of their own neighbourhood.

## 3 · The send-off

> **[Title card]** Sunday, 3 September 1967 · **Enlistment Day** · Queenstown Community Centre

> **[Fact]** On Sunday 3 September 1967, the first full-time national servicemen gathered at community centres across Singapore
> with their families, then left for camp in army lorries.

*(A band plays. A banner. Families at the gate. A military truck waits with its tailboard down. The MP hands each boy a
small gift. Mr. Rajan stands beside Ravi. Siti is there. Boon's spot, by the gate where he'd stand, is empty.)*

*(Design note: NAS photos from 3 Sep 1967 show recruits reporting at a community centre and leaving by military truck while families watch.
The captions don't say which intake. Present this as a typical send-off, not a record of the first batch.)*

**MR. RAJAN:** Ravi. Eat properly. Write to your mother.

**RAVI (18):** *(nods)*

**SITI (37):** *(to Farid)* He'll come round. He's Boon. He needs time to be wrong first.

*Kindness payoff — if Sparky helped tie the bundles in 1942:*

**NEIGHBOUR (now 50s):** The rope bear! Now you're going to be a soldier? Aiyoh. Eat well, ah.

*Optional kindness (Ch3):* *(An old Hokkien Ah Ma, Ah Hock's grandmother, stands alone, holding a paper bag of oranges.
She won't go near the truck.)*

**AH HOCK'S AH MA:** *(Hokkien, subtitled)* Good iron isn't made into nails… **[native review]**

*(If Sparky takes the oranges to the truck for her:)* **AH HOCK'S AH MA:** *(to Sparky)* Tell him to eat them. All of them. **[native review]**

*(Farid climbs into the truck. Sparky climbs up after him. Sparky looks back at the gate. Nobody there.)*

*Fact card — **Fifty dinners**: Many families were worried about their sons becoming soldiers. So community leaders held about fifty
send-off dinners for the first batch, and at some of them each young man got a medal.*

## 4 · Arrival — Taman Jurong Camp

> **[Title card]** Taman Jurong Camp. September 1967.

*(Five-storey blocks of one-room flats, turned into barracks in a hurry. The lorry drives in along the road in front of the blocks
and stops. Sergeant Osman is waiting by the tailboard.)*

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Down! Down from the lorry! Line up. One line. Here.

*(A beat later: five of them in a line in front of the lorry. Osman walks the line; the camera turns to each one as he speaks.)*

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** I am Sergeant Osman. For six months I am your mother, your father and your alarm clock.

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Names. Short. You.

**LEO:** Leonard Pereira, Sergeant! Leo. From Katong. I've seen every war film at the Capitol, so—

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Short, I said. Next.

**FARID (18):** Farid, Sergeant. Queenstown.

**AH HOCK:** *(in Hokkien)* …Ah Hock. **[native review]**

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** No English? No Malay? Hm. You will learn. Next.

**RAVI (18):** *(quietly)* Ravi.

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** *(looking down at Sparky)* And you… are very short. Never mind.

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Four boys who can't talk to each other, and a bear. My section.

*(Behind them, the lorry pulls away down the road.)*

## 4b · Day one — the first drill

> **[Title card]** The next morning.


*(The parade square. Five of them in a line: Leo, Farid, Sparky, Ah Hock, Ravi. In the background, two foreign advisers in straw
hats watch and take notes.)*

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Listen. I say two words. The first word is a warning. The second word, you move. Not before.

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** In this army we drill in Malay. Chinese, Malay, Indian, Eurasian, everybody learns the same words. Understand?

**LEO:** Yes, Sergeant! Completely, Sergeant!

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** You don't. But you will.

*Objective: Wait for the second word. Then move. (The first four commands show a picture.)*

*Drill (day one): Sedia · Ke kanan pusing · Ke kiri pusing · Ke belakang pusing. Every command shows its picture.*

*(Ah Hock can't follow the Malay. He watches Sparky's feet and copies him: right or wrong.)*

→ on time: **SERGEANT OSMAN:** Hm. Not bad.

→ jumped the gun: **SERGEANT OSMAN:** I said wait. Again!

→ wrong way: *(Ah Hock turns the wrong way with Sparky and they bump.)* **SERGEANT OSMAN:** Two boys, one mistake. Very efficient. Again!

*(Scripted, the third command: Leo turns before the executive word.)* **SERGEANT OSMAN:** Pereira. This is not a race. Nobody is chasing you.

*(After two tries on the same command:)* **SERGEANT OSMAN:** Close enough. Next.

*(End of the drill. The section is facing four different ways.)*

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Four boys, four directions.

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** *(to Sparky)* And you. Tomorrow, stand on a box.

*Look closer:* *The men in straw hats:* Two advisers taking notes. — photo card: **The "Mexicans"** — Israeli military advisers helped
train Singapore's first soldiers. To keep it secret, people called them "Mexicans". Some wore straw hats.

## 5 · Rifles — some weeks later

> **[Title card]** Some weeks later. Weapons training.

*(The parade square. A wooden rack of M16s between Sergeant Osman and the section.)*

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** This is the M16. From today it goes where you go.

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** You will know it better than your mother's face. Take one. Gently.

*Objective: Take a rifle from the rack. (Everyone takes one; they stand at order arms, butt by the right foot.)*

**LEO:** It's lighter than in the films.

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Pereira. Everything is lighter than in the films.

*Drill: Sandang… SENJATA! (sling arms). The rifles go up to the shoulder.* **[SAF/native review]**

**SERGEANT OSMAN:** Good. Now it goes where you go. Even to the parade.

*Fact card — **The M16**: In the late 1960s the Singapore Armed Forces adopted the American M16 rifle. Singapore later made its own
under licence, as the M16S1. In this game the rifles are only for drill.*

## 5b · Farid's letter

*(Farid and Ah Hock by Farid's bed, sharing Boon's tiffin carrier: four tins of rice, chicken, egg and achar. Ah Hock stays in

**AH HOCK:** *(in Hokkien, eating from the tiffin carrier)* Wah. Who made this? **[native review]**

**FARID (18):** Abang Boon. He's angry with me. He still cooks like this when he's angry.

**FARID (18):** He hasn't written. Not once.

**FARID (18):** Sparky. You start. What do I say first?

## Album & credits (chapter 3 and ending)

**Drill words** card: the table at the top of this script.

**Real vs imagined:**
- Imagined: Farid, Ah Hock, Ravi, Leo Pereira, Sergeant Osman, Ah Hock's Ah Ma, Irfan, and Boon's kopitiam.
- Real: in 1967 about 9,000 young men born in the first half of 1949 registered for National Service. About 900 were chosen for full-time
  service in two new battalions, 3 SIR and 4 SIR, at Taman Jurong Camp. They enlisted at the Central Manpower Base in Kallang in August 1967.
- Real: their passing-out parade was at Taman Jurong Camp on Saturday, 2 March 1968. Minister Lim Kim San took the salute. His words in
  this chapter are quoted from a 2017 speech by DPM Tharman Shanmugaratnam, who repeated them.
- Real: many families were unhappy about sons becoming soldiers. A Chinese saying goes "good iron isn't made into nails, good men don't
  become soldiers". Community leaders held send-off dinners and ceremonies to win families over.
- Real: the recruits spoke many languages. One first-batch recruit remembered a buddy who spoke neither English nor Malay, so they used sign language.
- Likely, not certain: drill commands were in Malay, as they are today. No 1967 record we found says so.
- Real: Israeli advisers helped train the first soldiers, and people called them "Mexicans" to keep it secret.
- Real: the Temasek green uniform, starched stiff enough to stand up. Recruits carried their own beds up the barrack stairs (from one account).
- **Not known:** whether any Malay Singaporeans were among the first 900. In the early years of National Service, many Malay Singaporeans were
  not called up. Today every fit young man is called up, whatever his race or religion. Farid is an imagined character.
- Partly known: the SAF adopted the M16 rifle in the late 1960s. Which rifles the very first intake carried at their passing-out
  parade is not confirmed; the game shows M16s, carried for drill only.
- Real: the camp site is now Taman Jurong Greens, a park, with a heritage marker put up in 2017.
