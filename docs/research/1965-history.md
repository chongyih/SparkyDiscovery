# Chapter 2 research: Singapore, Monday 9 August 1965

A fact-check for the kopitiam scene set on Separation Day in Queenstown, with sources. Times are Singapore
local time. Where I could not confirm something from a primary source, it says **unverified**.

## 1. The Proclamation (9 Aug 1965)

- **Broadcast:** Radio Singapura, **10 am**, 9 Aug 1965. The Gazette was issued at the same time
  ([NAS record lky19650809a][nas-proc], [NAS photo record][nas-proc-img], [NLB Infopedia][nlb-sep]).
  Lee, in a 1982 oral history: "10 am, 9 August, [the announcement] went out. I decided not to announce it myself,
  just announce it by Gazette and by radio" ([BiblioAsia 2026, extract from *The Albatross File*][ba-albatross]).
- **Languages:** NAS holds the **Malay** reading, from Radio Singapura Channel II. The announcer reads Malay translations of Lee's
  Proclamation and of the Tunku's Proclamation. NAS says only this Malay recording has so far been found
  ([NAS audio 1997021116][nas-malay], [NAS Archivist Pick 2015][nas-pick]). Readings in the other services
  (English, Chinese, Tamil) are likely but **unverified**. Wikipedia names the English announcer as
  **Steven Lee**, citing ST 2015 reports. I did not verify this first-hand.
- **Paraphrase:** Every people has the right to be free. Malaysia was formed in 1963. On 7 Aug 1965 the two governments
  agreed Singapore would leave, and the Tunku has proclaimed this. So Lee Kuan Yew, for the people and Government of Singapore,
  declares that from today Singapore is independent.
- **Key sentence (checked against the signed original, NAS image 20140000018):** "...that as from today the ninth day
  of August in the year one thousand nine hundred and sixty-five Singapore shall be forever a sovereign democratic and
  independent nation, founded upon the principles of liberty and justice and ever seeking the welfare and happiness of
  her people in a more just and equal society." (The NAS typed transcript drops the "a". The signed original has it.)
  Drafted by Law Minister E. W. Barker. It was typed on ordinary PMO letterhead and still has staple rust.

## 2. Lee Kuan Yew's press conference (9 Aug 1965)

- **Where and when:** "at the Broadcasting House, Singapore, at 12.00 p.m. on Monday, August 9, 1965"
  (title of the NAS transcript). An NLB photo caption places it in "the temporary studio at Caldecott Hills". His
  Mandarin tutor and translator Foong Choon Hon sat beside him ([NAS transcript record][nas-pc], [PDF][nas-pc-pdf],
  [NLB photo][nlb-pc-img]).
- **Length:** the NAS audiovisual record "Prime Minister Meets The Press" runs **1:03:28** (recorded duration).
  Broadcast/release date: 09/08/1965 ([NAS AV record][nas-av]).
- **Televised:** yes, on the same day. The exact air time is unverified (see the table above).
- **He broke down.** The transcript marks: "(RECORDING WAS STOPPED FOR THE PRIME MINISTER TO REGAIN HIS COMPOSURE)".
  The transcript gives **no length** for the pause. The popular "20 minutes" figure is **unverified**. I could not find it in any
  primary source.
- **Exact wording (NAS transcript lky19650809b, verbatim including its punctuation):**
  - (a) "And I would like to add one...  You see, this is a moment of ... everytime we look back on this moment when we
    signed this agreement which severed Singapore from Malaysia, it will be a moment of anguish.  For me, it is a moment
    of anguish because all my life.... you see, the whole of my adult life.... I have believed in Merger and the unity of
    these two territories. You know, it's a people, connected by geography, economics, and ties of kinship.....
    Would you mind if we stop for a while."
  - (b) and (c), near the end, addressed "really to the people of Singapore": "There is nothing to be worried about it.
    Many things will go on just as usual.  But be firm, be calm. We are going to have a multi-racial nation in Singapore.
    We will set the example.  This is not a Malay nation;  this is not a Chinese nation; this is not an Indian nation.
    Everybody will have his place:  equal; language, culture, religion."
  - Closing line: "And finally, let us, really Singaporeans -- I cannot call myself a Malaysian now -- we unite,
    regardless of race, language, religion, culture."
  - On water, in the same passage: "the water agreement is firm and solid, and guaranteed."
  - There is **no** "Singapore shall be..." line in the press conference. That wording is from the Proclamation (section 1).
- **Footage holder and credit:** NAS holds the recording (accession 1997002660). The NAS player credits it
  "**Courtesy of Mediacorp Pte Ltd**", so Mediacorp (successor to RTS) owns the copyright, and reuse needs written permission.
  Suggested credit: "Prime Minister Meets The Press (9 Aug 1965). Courtesy of Mediacorp Pte Ltd / National Archives
  of Singapore." NAS stills are credited "Ministry of Information and the Arts Collection, courtesy of National Archives of
  Singapore".

## 3. Newspapers

- *The Straits Times*, **Tuesday 10 August 1965, p. 1**: **"Singapore is out"**, by Felix Abisheganaden (Kuala Lumpur).
  Other front-page items: "Tengku: It was my idea", "What it means—at a glance", "Raja is named Foreign Minister",
  and a leader, "Now look to the future" ([NewspaperSG issue][st-0810], [article][st-out]).
- *The Straits Times* of 9 Aug 1965 was a morning paper printed before the news broke. I found **no evening English paper**, and whether
  there were Chinese or Malay evening extras on 9 Aug is **unverified**.

## 4. Radio, Rediffusion and TV

- **Radio Singapura** (named in 1959) stayed in Singapore when Radio Malaysia formed in 1963. After independence it merged with
  Television Singapura into **Radio Television Singapura (RTS)** under the Ministry of Culture ([Roots: Celebrating Radio][roots-radio]).
  In 1965 it broadcast in English, Malay, Chinese (Mandarin and dialects) and Tamil. Channel II was Malay ([NAS][nas-malay]).
  One secondary source gives Malay, Chinese and English from 6 am to midnight and Indian (Tamil) services from 6 am to 9 pm. **Unverified.**
- **Rediffusion** (wired radio, launched 1 Aug 1949, $5 a month): "In the 1960s and '70s, the station's Chinese dialect
  programmes enjoyed a strong following, and many coffee shops and households were fitted with Rediffusion sets." It had about
  **50,000 subscribers by the 1960s**. The famous storytellers were Lee Dai Sor (Cantonese), Ng Chia Kheng (Teochew), Ong Toh (Hokkien)
  and Chong Soon Fat (Hakka) ([NLB Rediffusion][nlb-redif]). A Rediffusion box on a kopitiam wall is very accurate.
- **TV:** Television Singapura launched on **15 Feb 1963**. About **8% of households** had a set then, and there were **50 public viewing
  points** at places like community centres. Channel 5 was English and Malay. Channel 8 started Nov 1963 with Mandarin and Tamil
  ([BiblioAsia: Singapore TV][ba-tv]). I found no 1965 household figure, so TV was probably still uncommon. A coffee shop
  with its own set is plausible but not documented here.

## 5. Public reaction and worries

- **Shock and regret:** the ST leader of 10 Aug says "the first reaction... is one of cruel shock and profound regret". By 11 Aug
  the ST leader ("Foes and Friends") describes the mood as "grief and regret" and accepts there was no practical alternative.
- **Firecrackers:** ST 10 Aug, "Cracker-firing": shortly after "the 10 a.m. radio announcement... sporadic bursts
  of crackerfiring were reported in various parts of Chinatown" ([NewspaperSG issue][st-0810]).
- **Relief and business:** the Chinese Chamber of Commerce president welcomed it. Trade-with-China and Indonesia traders
  "rejoiced". Bank of China staff were all smiles, because a federal closure order was reversed. The stock exchange had its busiest day of the year.
- **Normal life:** Causeway traffic and Johor buses ran normally ("Johore Causeway traffic movement normal"). Overseas phone
  lines were jammed. Civil-defence national servicemen were told not to report for training "until further notice".
- **Queenstown local colour:** residents of **Commonwealth Crescent and Close** planned a welcome buffet party for about 6,000
  people with Lee and Labour Minister Jek Yeun Thong on Saturday 14 Aug (ST 11 Aug 1965, "6,000 will attend party for Lee") ([issue][st-0811]).
- **Jobs:** unemployment "was close to 9%" in 1965 ([MAS, Ravi Menon 2015][mas-econ]). Other sources say about 10%. Use "about one in ten".
- **Konfrontasi was still going on.** The MacDonald House bombing (Orchard Road, 10 Mar 1965) killed 3 and injured at least 33. There were at least 42
  Konfrontasi attacks in Singapore from 1963 to 1966. It ended in Aug 1966 ([Roots][roots-mh], [SG101][sg101-def]). On 10 Aug Indonesia's Subandrio said
  confrontation would continue.
- **British bases:** they stayed. Britain announced its withdrawal on 16 Jan 1968, and the Far East Command left with parades on 29 and 31 Oct 1971
  ([NLB British withdrawal][nlb-brit], [SG101][sg101-bw]).
- **Family in Malaysia:** many families had relatives across the Causeway. On 9 Aug Dr Toh said the Singapore end of the Causeway would
  "always be open to Malaysians".

## 6. Water

- Yes, the water agreements were guaranteed. **Section 14** of the Constitution and Malaysia (Singapore Amendment) Act 1965 is headed
  "Mutual government guarantees of water agreements". That Act is Annex B to the Independence of Singapore (Separation) Agreement of 7 Aug 1965,
  and it is often cited loosely as "Article 14 of the Separation Agreement". It says Singapore guarantees that the PUB will abide by "the Water Agreements dated
  1st September, 1961, and 29th September, 1962", and Malaysia guarantees that Johor will too ([Wikisource Annex B][ws-annexb], [MFA][mfa-water]).
  Section 13 lets the UK keep its bases. Article V of the main agreement covers joint defence.

## 7. Defence

- **Two regular battalions only:** 1 SIR (formed 1957) and 2 SIR (1963, per NLB; SG101 says 1962), plus volunteers
  ([NLB: Singapore Army established][nlb-army]).
- **On 9 Aug 1965** both were under the Malaysian 4th Federal Infantry Brigade. NLB: "many of the men, particularly those from 2 SIR,
  were in the midst of being deployed to different parts of Malaysia, including Sabah". SG101 says 2 SIR went to Sabah "a week after"
  separation. Singapore only had full control from **Jan 1966**, when 2 SIR came home and found 1 Royal Malay Regiment occupying its Camp
  Temasek at Ulu Pandan. The men had to camp in tents at Farrer Park. Exactly where 1 SIR was on 9 Aug is **unverified**.
- **The Singapore Army Bill** passed on 23 Dec 1965. National Service came in 1967 (see `ww2-history.md`).

## 8. State symbols

- The flag, state crest (coat of arms) and anthem *Majulah Singapura* (composed 1958 by Zubir Said) were **unveiled on 3 Dec 1959**, at
  Yusof Ishak's installation as Yang di-Pertuan Negara ([MCCY][mccy]). So in 1965 they were already familiar. The date the Legislative
  Assembly formally adopted them (Nov 1959) is **unverified**.

## 9. Currency

- On 9 Aug 1965 people used the **Malaya and British Borneo dollar**, issued since 1953–54 by the Board of Commissioners of Currency,
  Malaya and British Borneo. Singapore's own currency board started on **12 June 1967** and issued the **Orchid series**
  ([NLB currency history][nlb-cur], [MAS][mas-cur]). Nice link: the 1967 $1 Orchid note shows **Blocks 74–80 Commonwealth Drive,
  Queenstown** ([Roots][roots-blk75]). Coffee at "10 cents" or so is plausible but **unverified**.

## 10. The 1964 race riots

- **21 July 1964**, during a procession for the Prophet Muhammad's birthday: 23 dead and 454 injured, with a curfew until 2 Aug. **2 September 1964**,
  after a Malay trishaw rider was killed in Geylang Serai: 13 dead and 106 injured ([NLB Communal riots of 1964][nlb-riots]).
- Age-appropriate line: *"In 1964, fights broke out between Chinese and Malay people in Singapore. People were hurt and killed, and families had to stay
  indoors during curfews. Many neighbours sheltered and protected each other."* (The last sentence is widely told in oral histories but is not
  sourced here.)

## 11. Kopitiam drink lingo

- **kopi** (Malay "coffee") = coffee **with condensed milk and sugar** by default. **kopi-C** = evaporated milk and sugar. **kosong** =
  no sugar. **kopi gao** = strong ([BiblioAsia: Makan Places and Coffee Socks][ba-kopi]).
- **O** = Hokkien 烏 *o͘*, "black": kopi-O is black coffee **with sugar**. **peng** = Hokkien 冰, "ice". **teh** = Hokkien 茶 *tê*, "tea".
  **siew dai** = less sweet and **ga dai** = extra sweet. The usual explanation is Cantonese 少底 *siu dai* / 加底 *gaa dai*, "less or more base" (**origin disputed**).
  **Kosong** is Malay for "empty" or "zero" ([Wikipedia: Kopi][wiki-kopi]).
- **kopi-C, two origin claims (both unproven):** "C" for **Carnation** brand evaporated milk, or Hainanese 鮮 *si* ("fresh", as in fresh milk),
  which is the explanation coffee-shop association leaders give ([Makansutra][makansutra]).
- **1960s usage:** kopitiams were mostly Hainanese and Foochow-run (more than 80% by the 1950s), and "kopitiam" is a Malay-Hokkien blend
  ([Culturepaedia][ccc-kopi], [Roots: Beyond Kaya Toast][roots-kopi]). I could not date any of these terms to a 1960s source (the NewspaperSG
  search is blocked to automated access), so their 1960s use is **unverified**. Kopi, kopi-O, teh, kosong and peng are safe bets. Kopi-C, siew dai
  and ga dai may be later. Use them sparingly.
- **Setting:** 1960s coffee shops were multiracial spaces, alongside Malay *kedai kopi* and Indian *teh sarabat* stalls ([Roots][roots-kopi]).

## 12. Queenstown in 1965

- Blocks 45, 48 and 49 Stirling Road were **seven-storey rental blocks completed Oct 1960**, the first finished under the HDB. They were nicknamed *qik lao*
  ("seven storeys", Hokkien), and Bukit Ho Swee fire (1961) victims were resettled there ([Roots][roots-hdb]). Whether they had ground-floor shops is **unverified**.
- By 1962 the ten-storey blocks at **Commonwealth Drive / Tanglin Halt** (*chap lau chu*) were done, next to the Queenstown IV market ([NLB photo][nlb-qt4], [Roots][roots-blk75]).
  **Commonwealth Avenue Wet Market** opened 23 Oct 1960. Venus and Golden City theatres opened at Margaret Drive in late 1965. The Queenstown Combined Clinic
  at Margaret Drive dates from about 1963 ([Roots: Queenstown][roots-qt]). The Tanglin Halt provision shop Thin Huat dates from 1964 (secondary source).
- **Community centre:** Remember Singapore (a secondary source) lists an Alexandra CC at Prince Philip Avenue (1953), later renamed Queenstown CC. Whether a
  "Queenstown Community Centre" by that name existed in 1965 is **unverified**. Use "the community centre" without a name.
