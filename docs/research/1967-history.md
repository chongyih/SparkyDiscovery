# Chapter research: the first National Service intake, 1967

A fact-check for the 1967 National Service scenes (enlistment, Taman Jurong Camp, passing out), with sources.
Where I could not confirm something from a primary source, it says **unverified**. Secondary sources
(Remember Singapore, Wikipedia, blogs, Wonderwall, Mothership) are labelled as such and used only as leads.

Verdict key: **CONFIRMED** (primary or official source), **LIKELY** (strong indirect evidence, or an official-adjacent
secondary source), **UNVERIFIED** (no source found either way), **FALSE** (a source contradicts it).

## Changes to the design (read this first)

| Assumption | Finding | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~9,000 men born 1 Jan–30 Jun 1949 registered | **Confirmed, with a small correction.** 9,428 were expected and about 96% registered. One Roots page says "30 July 1949". That is a typo; NLB says 30 June. | Say "about 9,000". |
| The 900 reported to Taman Jurong Camp on 17 Aug | **Partly wrong.** They enlisted at the **Central Manpower Base (CMPB), Kallang**, from 17 to 25 Aug 1967. 250 enlisted on the first morning. Formal training at Taman Jurong began on 11 Sep. | Put the enlistment desk at CMPB Kallang, not at the camp gate. |
| Five-storey JTC one-room flats | **Confirmed** by the PMO and NLB. One government-adjacent site says "seven-storey" HDB flats, which clashes. JTC was only set up in 1968, so "JTC flats" is probably today's label for them. | Use five storeys. Say "one-room flats" and leave out the builder. |
| A passing-out parade existed | **Confirmed: Saturday 2 March 1968, Taman Jurong Camp.** Defence Minister **Lim Kim San** (not Goh Keng Swee) took the salute. Goh, by then Finance Minister, attended. | Use 2 Mar 1968, with Lim Kim San reviewing. |
| Goh Keng Swee as Defence Minister at enlistment | **Wrong from 17 Aug 1967.** Lim Kim San became Minister for the Interior and Defence on 17 Aug 1967, the day enlistment began. | Refer to "the Minister" or Lim Kim San for anything after mid-August 1967. Goh can appear as the architect of NS. |
| Farid (Malay, born 1949) enlists in the first intake | **Unverified and risky.** No record found of Malays in 3 or 4 SIR's first intake. Lee Kuan Yew said in 2001 that "many Malay Singaporeans were not called up" at first. He also said there was "no blanket ban". | Don't make Farid one of the 900. See section 3 for alternatives. |
| Drill commands in Malay | **Likely, but no 1967 primary source found.** | Keep a few core Malay commands. Don't claim the exact 1967 wording. |

## 1. Passing out and the end of basic training

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** A passing-out parade was held at Taman Jurong Camp on **Saturday 2 March 1968**.

- **Date and place:** NAS has 24 government photos captioned "Passing out parade of national servicemen at Taman Jurong Camp",
  covering date 02/03/1968 ([NAS photo][nas-pop]). *Eastern Sun*, Sun 3 Mar 1968, dateline "Sat": Lim Kim San "this afternoon
  took the salute at the passing out parade of National Servicemen held at the Taman Jurong camp" ([Eastern Sun issue][es-680303]).
- **Who reviewed it:** Lim Kim San, Minister for the Interior and Defence. NAS captions show him inspecting contingents, reviewing
  the march-past, speaking, and "mingling with guests" ([NAS][nas-pop-guests], [NAS][nas-pop-speak]).
  **Dr Goh Keng Swee**, "Minister of Finance", was there, talking to an armed forces official ([NAS][nas-pop-goh]).
  Minister of State for Defence **Wee Toon Boon** also attended ([NAS][nas-pop-wee]).
- **What was said:** DPM Tharman quoted Lim's words at the parade in 2017: "You are the pioneers in a new field and pioneers in a most
  honourable way of serving the country" ([PMO 2017][pmo-tharman]). The *Eastern Sun* headline was "WELL DONE, says Kim San to call-ups". It
  reported that he told the recruits "events had moved fast since Aug 1967". *The Straits Times*, 4 Mar 1968: "A good soldier is a good citizen, says Minister" ([ST issue][st-680304]).
- **Families:** NAS has a photo titled "Spectators at passing out parade of national servicemen in Taman Jurong Camp" ([NAS][nas-pop-spect]),
  and others of Lim with "guests". That the spectators were families is **likely but unverified**. No caption says "parents".
- **Length of basic training:** formal training began on 11 Sep 1967 ([MINDEF][mindef-hist]), so the parade came about **25 weeks
  (roughly six months)** later. This is my inference. I found no source that states the length.
- **Other milestones:**
  - Straight after, on 3 Mar 1968, the *Eastern Sun* reported "Dinners for call-ups". National Servicemen "enlisted after August/September last year will be guests of honour at
    dinner parties to be held this month at all constituencies" ([Eastern Sun issue][es-680303]).
  - **National Day Parade, 9 Aug 1968:** Roots lists "The debut of our first batch of national servicemen marching at the NDP" as a 1968 first.
    That was the famous rain-soaked "Rugged Society" parade ([Roots: A March in August][roots-ndp]). **CONFIRMED.**
  - **Into the reserve, Aug 1969.** MINDEF says August marks the first batch's enlistment "and their subsequent entry into the Reserve" ([MINDEF NS50 fact sheet][mindef-ns50fs]).
    Tharman: "emerging two years later, melded together" ([PMO][pmo-tharman]).
- **Minister change:** Lim Kim San was Minister for the Interior and Defence from 1967 to 1968 ([MINDEF past leadership][mindef-past]). Wikipedia gives the start date as
  17 Aug 1967 ([Wikipedia][wiki-lks], secondary).

**Suggestions for the script:** Set the finale at the 2 Mar 1968 parade at Taman Jurong: march-past, the minister taking the salute, spectators
in the stands. Families watching is a reasonable dramatisation, but don't label them as documented. Quote Lim's "pioneers in a new field" line; it is sourced.
The 1968 NDP in the rain works as an epilogue.

## 2. Language: drill commands and instruction

**Malay drill commands in 1967: LIKELY.** **Recruits speaking many languages, with real barriers: CONFIRMED.**

- **Today:** CMPB tells enlistees that basic commands "will be given in Malay" ([CMPB][cmpb-drill]). The 1967 primary sources I could reach
  never say what language drill was in.
- **1967 claim:** Wonderwall.sg (a government-adjacent secondary source) says "during training, drill commands were given in Malay" and that "English lessons were conducted"
  because "most of the new recruits at the time didn't speak English" ([Wonderwall][wonderwall]). The second part doesn't sit well
  with NLB, which says the full-time men were "about 10 percent of the better-educated" ([NLB][nlb-compulsory]). Many of them were probably Chinese-educated rather than English-educated.
  **Unverified.**
- **Oral history (NAS, primary):** Albel Singh, born 1949, "recalled his enlistment into 3 Singapore Infantry Regiment (SIR)". His BMT was in Taman Jurong.
  "His buddy was a farmer who could not speak English and Malay. They communicated in sign language." ([NAS OH 004356][nas-oh-albel]). The
  synopsis mentions both English and Malay, which hints that those were the working languages. Whether Singh was in the Aug 1967 intake is **likely but unverified**. 3 SIR and a 1949
  birth year fit.
- **Instructor's view:** SWO (Ret) Ong Hui Pheng, instructor to the first batch at 3 SIR: "People spoke many different languages and dialects; some were
  not prepared to serve" ([Pioneer, NS50][pioneer-ns50]).
- **The instructors themselves were mixed.** A NAS photo of 3 Aug 1967 shows "SAFTI men who will command and instruct recruits for two new battalions":
  Sgt Lee Kim Hong and 2LTs Ajit Singh, Quah Seng Kee, Dildar Singh Dhot, Ser Hoe Peng, Quek Chick Min and Javamani Stephen ([NAS][nas-safti-instr]).
- **Hokkien:** Remember Singapore (secondary) says Hokkien was common in SAF training and "banned in October 1978" ([Remember Singapore][rs-ns]). **Unverified.**
  A 1970 teacher's memoir says male teachers were enlisted to teach English to "Hokkien peng" ([Thirst.sg][thirst], secondary). That was a later intake, not 1967.
- **Command list:** the common forms are *sedia* (attention), *senang diri* (stand at ease), *ke kanan pusing* (right turn), *ke belakang pusing* (about turn),
  *dari kiri, cepat jalan* (quick march) and *berhenti* (halt). Source: a Remember Singapore reader comment, secondary and undated ([Remember Singapore][rs-ns]). Note that
  today's form of the right turn is usually "ke kanan pusing", not "kanan pusing". **Native or SAF review recommended.**

**Suggestions for the script:** Use Malay commands (*Sedia! Senang diri!*) on the parade square, and show a Hokkien- or Teochew-speaking recruit
lost at first and copying his neighbour. The sign-language buddy is a documented anecdote you can adapt. Have instructors explain in English
and mix in dialect. Avoid "Kanan pusing" unless a reviewer confirms it.

## 3. Malay recruits in the first intake

**Malays among the first 900: UNVERIFIED.** **Limited Malay call-up in the early years: CONFIRMED** (Lee Kuan Yew, 2001).

- **Official account (primary):** Senior Minister Lee Kuan Yew, dialogue with AMP and Majlis Pusat, 2 Mar 2001:
  "In the early days, many Malay Singaporeans were not called up for NS. When NS started in 1967, race relations were fragile and tenuous,
  after the riots of 1964 and separation in 1965." He added that Israeli instructors "would have been involved in the training of Malay/Muslim servicemen at a
  time of Muslim-Israeli conflict". "So we did not recruit every Malay male, unless we were confident his participation in NS would not be a problem. Even then there
  was no blanket ban against Malays in the SAF." And: "We now call up all those fit for national service, whatever his race or religion." ([NAS press release][lky-2001]).
- **Later statement:** in 1987, BG Lee Hsien Loong said the SAF did not want soldiers in a position where "his emotions for the nation may come into conflict with his
  emotions for his religion" (quoted in secondary sources. I did not see the primary text.)
- **Dates of the policy (secondary):** Wikipedia says Malays were "de facto" not required to serve from 1967 until 1977. It cites Alon Peled, *A Question of
  Loyalty* (1993), which I did not check ([Wikipedia][wiki-ns]). **Unverified.** MINDEF has since pointed to Malay pilots, commandos and air-defence personnel.
- **Malay Singaporeans in uniform in the 1960s:** yes, as regulars. The two pre-NS battalions were mixed, and Goh Chok Tong said "Only half the soldiers were
  Singaporeans" ([NAS, PM Goh 2002][gct-2002]). NAS holds an oral history by Mohamed Jamil Mohamed Amin on the "early days of SAFTI" ([NAS OH 004508][nas-oh-jamil]).
  Mohd Salleh Marican, born 1949 and from a Malay-language schooling background, says he served full-time NS "in the second batch", posted to SAFTI, Pasir Laba
  ([NAS OH 004832][nas-oh-salleh]). I did not check how he identifies ethnically, so don't cite him as "a Malay recruit".
- **What the first intake looked like:** DPM Tharman: "Young men, different races, different social backgrounds, thrown together" ([PMO][pmo-tharman]).
  The documented examples I found are Chinese, Sikh (Albel Singh) and Eurasian (Maurice Alphonso, named by Tharman as one of the pioneer batch).
- **Registration applied to all.** Every man born 1 Jan–30 Jun 1949 had to register (Mar–Apr 1967), and evaders faced arrest ([NLB][nlb-early]). So Farid would
  have registered. Whether he would have been posted full-time, part-time or not at all is **unverified**.

**Suggestions for the script:** Keep this neutral and accurate. Options, safest first:
1. Farid registers in March 1967 like everyone else, is **not** among the 900 full-timers, and is at the send-off dinner or the passing-out parade
   as a friend. A line such as "Not everyone was called up for full-time service in the first batch" is true and needs no explanation of the policy.
2. Farid is a **regular** soldier or a SAFTI trainee rather than a conscript. Malays did serve as regulars.
3. If Farid must be a conscript, move him to a later intake and a different unit, and don't claim he was one of the first 900.
Avoid dialogue that states a policy reason. If an older character reflects on it, paraphrase Lee's "fragile and tenuous" explanation and "no blanket ban".

## 4. Taman Jurong Camp, then and now

**Verdict: location CONFIRMED (Taman Jurong Greens). Marker design and text: UNVERIFIED.**

- **Where:** NHB places the "Former Taman Jurong Camp" at **Taman Jurong Greens** (coordinates 1.3371603, 103.7185363) ([Roots][roots-camp]).
  Tharman spoke "in this neighbourhood park, at the very site of the old Taman Jurong Camp" ([PMO][pmo-tharman]). Remember Singapore (secondary) says the camp was bounded by
  **Corporation Road, Corporation Drive and Yung An Road**, and that it is now the park ([Remember Singapore][rs-tj]). In 1967, Corporation Road was probably still
  **Jalan Peng Kang**. NHB says Jalan Peng Kang "was renamed Corporation Road in the 1970s" ([NHB Jurong trail booklet][nhb-booklet]).
- **What it was:** "several five-storey blocks of 1-room flats built by JTC were converted in great hurry into military barracks" ([PMO][pmo-tharman]).
  An NLB photo caption: "At the background are blocks of 5-storey buildings, formerly rental flats built by the Jurong Town Corporation." The camp "was home to the
  1 SIR (1968-1969), 3 SIR, 4 SIR and the General Artillery Headquarters (1966-1976)". 1 SIR was there from 16 Aug 1968 to 8 Jan 1969 ([NLB photo][nlb-img-parade]).
  The People's Defence Force started there in 1966, and the first artillery NSmen enlisted there in Feb 1968 ([PMO][pmo-tharman]).
- **Builder caveat:** JTC was formed in 1968 and took over Jurong's flats in 1969. The NHB booklet credits HDB with the first Taman Jurong flats (1963)
  ([NHB booklet][nhb-booklet]). So "JTC flats" is how today's sources describe them, and in 1967 they were probably HDB-built. **Unverified.**
- **Photos to align against:**
  - NLB, "Parade at the Taman Jurong Camp, 1960s": troops in ranks on an open parade ground, several long five-storey slab blocks with open corridors behind them, and folding
    chairs in the foreground ([NLB][nlb-img-parade]).
  - NLB, "Passing-out parade at Taman Jurong Camp of 20 PDF, 1966" ([NLB][nlb-img-pdf]).
  - NAS, 24 photos of the 2 Mar 1968 passing-out parade, including march-past and spectator shots ([NAS][nas-pop]). All MOIA photos are "Use and reproduction only with
    permission", credit "Ministry of Information and the Arts Collection, courtesy of National Archives of Singapore".
- **The 2017 marker:** placed on 6 Aug 2017 by DPM Tharman at a National Day observance ceremony, "on this field" ([PMO][pmo-tharman]). It is part of NHB's Jurong
  Heritage Trail ([Roots][roots-camp]). I could not find a photo or the wording of the marker, so its **look, text and exact spot are unverified**.
  An ST article (Toh Wen Li, 6 Aug 2017, "Heritage marker unveiled at site of old Taman Jurong Camp to honour pioneer NSmen") probably describes it, but I could not open it.
- **Layout:** Remember Singapore's commenters and a heritage blog say Corporation Drive split the camp into two parts (secondary). **Unverified.**

**Suggestions for the script:** The blocks are gone, so a then-and-now scene can't line up buildings. Line up the **open field** instead: the 1960s parade ground
against today's park lawn. Use the NLB "Parade at the Taman Jurong Camp" photo as the "then" frame, with ranks in front and slab blocks behind.
Build a generic heritage marker, don't copy its text, and have someone visit the park to photograph it before you finalise.

## 5. Enlistment day, 17 Aug 1967, and the days around it

**Verdict: CMPB Kallang enlistment CONFIRMED. Community-centre send-offs with trucks CONFIRMED (3 Sep 1967). Day-one details LIKELY (secondary).**

- **Where they reported:** *Eastern Sun*, 17 Aug 1967: "Enlistment of National Servicemen for full-time service in the Singapore Army begins tomorrow and will continue
  till Aug. 25 at the Central Manpower Base, Kallang" ([Eastern Sun issue][es-670817]). 18 Aug: "The first batch of 250 youths this morning enlisted for fulltime two year
  National Service", with the rest to follow by 25 Aug ([Eastern Sun issue][es-670818]).
- **Dinners:** *ST*, 18 Aug 1967, "Dinners to honour S'pore's first batch of soldiers": "Fifty dinners have been planned". "Their hosts will be the Republic's community leaders." The
  "about 900" "began reporting for service this morning" ([ST issue][st-670818]).
- **Send-off dinners (NAS photos, primary):** Tanjong Pagar CC (29 Aug), Waterloo Street with DPM Toh Chin Chye (30 Aug), Hong Lim CC and Siglap CC (31 Aug), where ministers presented a
  **medal** to each serviceman, Macpherson CC (1 Sep), and Pek Kio CC (2 Sep): "National Service recruits and their families attending the National Servicemen send-off dinner"
  ([NAS][nas-dinner-fam]). Mrs Devan Nair told youths at a Moulmein send-off to "be proud to serve" ([Eastern Sun, 3 Sep][es-670903]). Lim Kim San spoke at a Cairnhill CC send-off
  on 4 Sep ([Eastern Sun, 5 Sep][es-670905]).
- **Leaving from the community centre, 3 Sep 1967 (NAS, primary):** "National Service recruits reporting at Pek Kio Community Centre during their enlistment day" ([NAS][nas-reporting]),
  then a briefing, then "boarding a military truck" ([NAS][nas-truck]), and "Families bidding farewell to their sons, who are departing for National Service" ([NAS][nas-farewell]).
  At Telok Ayer CC, Education Minister Ong Pang Boon gave gifts, fruit and a banner, and a band played ([NAS][nas-telokayer], [BiblioAsia][ba-ns50]).
  **Which intake this was is unverified.** It was eight days before formal training began, and the two part-time groups enrolled at CMPB instead (*ST*, 4 Sep, for the
  Vigilante Corps: [ST issue][st-670904]). So it was **likely** full-time army men heading to camp, but no caption says "3 SIR" or "4 SIR".
- **Transport:** military trucks from CCs (above). Remember Singapore (secondary) says families "would line up along the roads to witness their departures on the three-tonner trucks"
  in the late 1960s and early 1970s ([Remember Singapore][rs-ns]). How men got from CMPB Kallang to Jurong in mid-August is **unverified**.
- **Day one in camp (secondary):** Wonderwall says the men "had to carry their own bed, cupboard, and equipment up the stairs to their bunk. There were no mattresses, just thin
  rubber sheets to sleep on." Training started "at 6am sharp", with "drills, exercises, and more drills - until 9pm!" ([Wonderwall][wonderwall]). **Likely**, but I found no primary
  source for it. Albel Singh's oral history covers "His first taste of army food" and "His family's reaction after his first trip home since enlisting" ([NAS OH][nas-oh-albel]); the full recording is open access.
- **Haircut, kit list, what they brought:** **unverified.** NLB lists a 1967 handbook, *The Army and You: Orientation Handbook*, that probably covers this ([BiblioAsia][ba-ns50]).
- **Uniform:** the No. 4 in "Temasek Green cotton drill", so heavily starched it could stand up by itself ([Roots][roots-ns]). (Already known.)
- **Israeli advisers:** two NAS oral histories confirm the nickname: "Israelis were referred to as Mexicans due to secrecy" ([NAS OH 003506][nas-oh-foong]), and "The Israelis were
  called Mexicans and had on straw hats" ([NAS OH 004508][nas-oh-jamil]).

**Suggestions for the script:** Show two scenes. First, enlistment at CMPB Kallang, with papers and the oath (as the part-timers also did). Second, a community-centre send-off:
a dinner with a medal or gift from the MP, families at the gate, and recruits climbing into a military truck. Carrying a bed frame up five flights of stairs is a good beat,
but it rests on one secondary source, so keep it light. Leave out a head-shaving scene, or keep it generic.

## 6. Family attitudes and the campaign to change them

**Verdict: parental reluctance CONFIRMED. Government and community persuasion CONFIRMED.**

- **The proverb:** NLB says volunteering fell short partly because of "the widespread belief among the majority Chinese population that 'good sons do not become soldiers'"
  ([NLB][nlb-early]). PM Goh Chok Tong, 2002: "Every Chinese parent knew of the saying that as good steel does not become nails, so good sons do not become soldiers"
  ([NAS][gct-2002]). The Chinese form 好铁不打钉，好男不当兵 is the standard proverb (also found in the reverse order). I did not find it printed in a 1967 Singapore source.
- **Opposition:** the most serious protest was on 27 Mar 1967, when 300 people demonstrated at New Bridge Road, Rochor Canal Road and Kim Keat Road ([NLB][nlb-compulsory]). There were
  also Chinese middle-school protests on 28 Mar ([NLB][nlb-early]).
- **Support:** the University of Singapore Students' Union backed the call-up, and the Singapore Chinese Chamber of Commerce urged youths to register ([NLB][nlb-early]).
  PM Goh: "The Government enlisted the support of leaders of chambers of commerce, clan associations, and community and grassroots organisations" ([NAS][gct-2002]).
- **Tools used:** booklets, *National Service and You* (1967), in all four official languages ([BiblioAsia][ba-ns50]); open houses at army camps and send-off
  parties at CCs ([Mothership][mothership], secondary); 50 community-hosted dinners in Aug–Sep 1967 ([ST][st-670818]); medals and gifts presented by ministers
  (NAS photos, section 5); a second round of dinners in March 1968 ([Eastern Sun][es-680303]); and cadet corps in schools ([BiblioAsia][ba-ns50]).
- **Flexibility:** new postponement rules were gazetted on 15 Sep 1967 for "valid cases" ([NLB][nlb-early]).

**Suggestions for the script:** Give one parent (for example, a Hokkien-speaking grandmother) the proverb, in her own words, before the send-off. Then show
her at the CC dinner or the passing-out parade, softened by the ceremony. The shift from reluctance to pride fits the record, as long as it isn't universal: some
families stayed uneasy.

## 7. The M16 rifle (added 25 Sep 2026, for the user's rifle scene)

**Verdict: the SAF adopted the M16 in the late 1960s: LIKELY (secondary sources only). What the first intake carried: UNVERIFIED.**

- Secondary sources say that in the late 1960s the SAF adopted the AR-15/M16 as its service rifle, and that because the rifles were
  hard to get from the United States, Singapore bought a licence to make its own, the **M16S1** ([Wikipedia: SAR 80][wiki-sar80],
  [Wikipedia: SAR 21][wiki-sar21]). Wikipedia gives 1967 for the M16S1's introduction, which conflicts with licensed production
  starting later; treat the date as uncertain.
- The Army's own history page and the NS pages checked do not say which rifle the first intake trained with or carried at the
  2 March 1968 parade ([Singapore Army history][army-hist]).

**Suggestions for the script:** show M16s for drill only (no firing), and say on the real-vs-imagined page that the first intake's
parade rifles aren't confirmed. Check the NAS parade photographs (§1) for the rifles actually carried before any public release.
