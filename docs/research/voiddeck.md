# Research — present-day Queenstown HDB void deck (Prologue / Epilogue set)

Reference notes only. No photographs are embedded or traced: every texture in the set is drawn
procedurally in `tools/build_voiddeck.py`.

## Queenstown heritage (why this estate)
- Queenstown was Singapore's **first satellite town**. The Singapore Improvement Trust (SIT) planned
  it from 1953 and named it after Queen Elizabeth II's coronation. Neighbourhoods include Queen's
  Close, Duchess, Tanglin Halt, Dawson, Mei Ling, Strathmore, Commonwealth, Dover, Ghim Moh.
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queenstown,_Singapore> ·
  <https://www.roots.gov.sg/stories-landing/stories/queenstown-the-queen-of-housing-estates/story>
- **Blocks 45, 48, 49 Stirling Road** are among HDB's first blocks: seven storeys, finished Oct 1960.
  Locals called the area "Chit Lau" (Hokkien for "seven storeys").
  <https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/landmarks/my-queenstown-heritage-trail/the-first-hdb-blocks-the-hdb-terraces> ·
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45,_48_and_49_Stirling_Road>
- **Tanglin Halt / Commonwealth Drive**: the 10-storey slab blocks locals call *chap lau chu*
  ("ten-storey houses"), done by 1962. Blocks 74–80 Commonwealth Drive appeared on the 1967 Orchid
  Series $1 note. They were repainted in bright colours in 2001, got lifts in 1979, and most were
  cleared under SERS in the 2020s.
  <https://remembersingapore.org/2022/10/24/tanglin-halt-sers-redevelopment/> ·
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanglin_Halt> ·
  <https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/image-detail?cmsuuid=99da8d80-7be3-459e-8c18-ed10098f5e1b>
- **Butterfly Block** is **Blk 168A Queensway** (early 1970s), HDB's first curved block. Its twin
  curves look like butterfly wings. It is on Queensway, not Strathmore Avenue. **Forfar House** on
  Strathmore Ave (1956, 14 storeys) was once Singapore's tallest residential block. Its blue glass was
  reused at SkyVille/SkyTerrace @ Dawson, the present-day Queenstown blocks.
  <https://www.littledayout.com/queenstown-exploring-singapores-first-satellite-estate-with-kids/> ·
  <https://theoccasionaltraveller.com/dawson-and-alexandra-heritage-trail/> ·
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forfar_House>
- Queenstown is an ageing "elderly town" with many long-time residents, so a 91-year-old Mr. Boon
  sitting at his void deck is believable. HDB's 2023 Queenstown rejuvenation plan focuses on healthy
  living and active ageing.
  <https://www.hdb.gov.sg/cs/infoweb/about-us/news-and-publications/press-releases/30092023-Queenstown-ROH> ·
  <https://www.hdb.gov.sg/-/media/about-us/our-role/plan-and-design-towns/town-design-guides/Queenstown-Town-Design-Guide.pdf>

## The void deck
- It is the open ground floor under an HDB block: a column grid holding up the flats, with the
  **lift lobby and the letterbox bank** at the centre. Common extras are bicycle racks, vending
  machines, residents' corners and Senior Citizens' Corners (1990s–2000s), and CCTV. It is used for
  chess, weddings, funerals, kids' play and simply sitting.
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Void_deck> ·
  <https://thinkingcity.org/2018/11/21/the-void-deck-singapores-secret-community-infrastructure/>
- History: the first one was at Blk 26 Jalan Klinik (1963). It became standard after about 1969–73.
  E.W. Barker wanted sheltered play space for children in the rain. The term entered HDB reports in
  1977/78. The 1982 precinct system tied void decks to playgrounds.
  <https://mothership.sg/2016/12/the-story-behind-when-the-void-deck-was-introduced-and-how-it-was-invented/>
  (the design doc already notes void decks were not yet standard in 1965).
- Walls "smooth white" (in practice pale cream or grey paint), with a matte, non-slip grey floor.
  Walls carry "Do Not…" notices (no ball games, no cycling) and Town Council notice boards.
- Lighting: ceiling fluorescent tube battens (1×/2× 1.2 m tubes), now being replaced by LED battens
  of the same linear shape. So the "long thin cool-white light bar" still reads correctly in 2026.
  <https://gintai.wordpress.com/2012/10/02/from-fluorescent-lightings-to-led-lightings-in-all-hdb-flats/>
- Letterboxes: older blocks had rows of tall metal boxes, lockless, and residents added their own
  padlocks. Upgraded blocks have a **centralised grid of small lockable doors** (aluminium or grey
  powder coat) with a mail-slot band and a master-key door for the postman.
  <https://mustsharenews.com/hdb-void-deck-design/>
- Town Councils maintain the lifts, lighting, letterboxes, void decks and landscaping. Upgrading
  (NRP/HIP) brings new letterboxes, residents' corners, seating and lift-lobby tiling.
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_Renewal_Programme>

## The stone table (hero prop)
- Terrazzo or concrete tables with a **mosaic-tile chessboard** set into the top, ringed by stools.
  They date from the late 1970s, and many are now being replaced. Other variants: green table-tennis
  tables, crescent seats curved around a table, two-seat stone benches along walls.
  <https://thelongnwindingroad.wordpress.com/2012/04/12/voids-that-have-filled-our-lives/> ·
  <https://goodyfeed.com/10-things-in-old-hdb-void-decks-that-we-all-miss-af-now/> ·
  <https://mustsharenews.com/tables-stools-void-decks/> ·
  <https://coconuts.co/singapore/features/capturing-singapores-vanishing-sense-of-community-through-its-concrete-gathering-spots-photos/>
- An NLB PictureSG record, "Void deck chess table and seats", confirms this as a documented type.
  <https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/pictures/Details/a64be1ad-ca59-4986-9124-a093d2b905ae>
- Sizes: prop replicas are about 0.80 m high. Stools are 0.27–0.32 m across and 0.50 m high. In-game
  we use a round top Ø 0.90 m at 0.75 m, a pedestal column, and 4 drum stools Ø 0.34 m × 0.45 m.
  <https://www.thepropzcompany.com/iconic-void-deck-chess-tile-table-and-chairs-national-day-heritage-furniture-replica>

## Choices made for the set (general observation + the sources above)
- Bay 12 m × 10 m, soffit 3.0 m, about 0.6 m square pillars with chamfered edges on a ~6 m grid. The
  block number **"Blk 53"** is painted on the pillar faces (big dark-red numerals on a cream band,
  Queenstown-style). The ceiling has 2-tube battens.
- Floor: grey-beige homogeneous tiles (300 mm) in the bay. At the open edge a drain grating and a
  small step lead down to the grass verge.
- Lift lobby wall (back): two stainless lift doors with floor indicators, a tiled lobby with a
  darker skirting band, a letterbox bank (grid of small grey doors), a Town Council notice board
  (A4 notices, a dengue poster in the NEA style but invented text, a "No ball games" sign), a red
  fire-hose-reel cabinet and a switch box.
- Props: bicycles by a rail, potted plants (bougainvillea, money plant, pandan) that residents keep
  by pillars, a plastic chair, a green rubbish bin, a stone two-seat bench.
- Beyond the open side: grass verge, a rain tree and a flame-of-the-forest-like tree, a small
  playground (colourful, rubber floor), a covered linkway. Behind them, neighbouring **slab blocks**
  in pastel with repeating corridor bands, service bays and laundry poles, one of them a curved
  "butterfly" nod to Blk 168A.
- Light: late afternoon (~5.30 pm), sun low in the west, warm and raking across the grass and
  into the deck. The deck interior is lit coolly by the tubes, and the warm/cool contrast is the look.
