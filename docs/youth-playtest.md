# Short playtest: journal and dialogue

Run separate sessions with younger readers and teens, on the devices they normally use. Start with three to five players; use parent/guardian arrangements appropriate to your setting. Allow about 15 minutes per player. Do not coach unless they cannot continue.

## Tasks

1. Start a new game and help Uncle Tan get the TV working.
2. Open the journal after finding the antenna. Find the new memory and return to play.
3. During a conversation, go to the next page and return to the previous page.
4. Finish the chapter, then find out about homes or jobs in the journal.
5. On a phone, turn journal pages and return to the game. Rotate the screen once.

## Observe

- Can they identify the next action without help?
- Do they read or listen to a dialogue page before advancing? Is the pace frustrating?
- Does each voice clip match its visible words, with no clipped or borrowed words?
- Do they notice “Memory added” without losing track of their task?
- Can they find the journal, turn pages and close it without help?
- Can they read the text and reliably tap controls on their device?
- Do they recognise what is historical and what is Sparky's imagined story?

Record the task, device, approximate age group, first hesitation, mis-taps and assistance needed. Avoid inferring engagement from completion speed alone.

## Ask afterwards

“What happened today?” “What do you remember discovering?” “Was there anything you wanted to skip?” “Was anything hard to read or use?”

Change the most repeated obstacle first, then test again. These sessions have not yet been conducted; automated checks cannot establish whether kids enjoy the pacing or understand the history.
