# Chapter ambience

These OGG files are trimmed, filtered and looped from free field recordings by
`tools/build_soundscape.py` (needs `numpy`, `scipy` and `soundfile`; sources are
cached in ignored `artifacts/audio-sources/`). They are modern recordings used as
illustrative sound design, not archival sound of Singapore in 1965.

Levels: the broadcast plays at about −18 LUFS and Uncle Tan's voice at about −24.6 LUFS.
His clean modern recording sounds louder than the thin 1965 audio at equal loudness (see
`ARCHIVE_VOLUME_DB` and `VOICE_VOLUME_DB` in `game/interface.gd`). Beds are
built at about −22 LUFS and bird calls at −20 LUFS. `game/soundscape.gd` then places the
walking mix about 20 dB below dialogue, lower still during dialogue, tuning and the broadcast.

The TV static plays while tuning. It gets quieter as the dial nears the station and cuts
out when the picture locks. It is puzzle feedback, so the ambient toggle does not mute it.

In game, the street, cicadas and fan are positional loops. Koel and myna calls come
from random trees every 14–32 seconds. The neighbours' murmur fades out when the press
conference starts, and the other beds duck under the original speech. Pause → Ambient
sound toggles all of these, separately from the archive's Mute button and Uncle Tan's
voice controls.

## WWII chapter effects

`ww2-rumble.wav`, `ww2-siren.wav`, `ww2-impact.wav`, `ww2-aircraft.wav`, `ww2-shutter.wav`, `ww2-door.wav` and `ww2-water.wav` are original deterministic synthetic effects generated by `tools/build_ww2_audio.py` using Python’s standard library. These generated files contain no downloaded samples. The gameplay siren uses the credited modern recording below. The WWII mix also reuses the credited Kenney footsteps and the existing neighbours recording at low volume in the shelter. All layers respect the chapter sound and pause controls. The synthetic effects accompany a fictional incident; they are not reconstructions of particular equipment. Playback is kept below full scale, with captions, a sound toggle and reduced visual effects in the chapter. The blast, pickup and shelter staging play through without skip or pause controls. The siren loops independently of the impact, ducks beneath it, continues quietly outdoors, is low-pass filtered inside the shelter, and fades out over 6.5 seconds during the water handoff. Its private filter bus is removed when the chapter exits.


### Recorded siren

`ww2-siren-recording.ogg`: “Civil-defense-siren-waver.ogg” by Techtonic, recorded in St. Paul, Minnesota on 5 November 2008. Released into the public domain by its creator. Source and license: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Civil-defense-siren-waver.ogg (accessed 23 September 2026).

`ww2-siren-recorded-loop.wav` is the gameplay version: seconds 8–32, converted to mono 22.05 kHz, loudness adjusted, with short edge fades. It replaces the synthetic siren in gameplay and retains the impact ducking, shelter filtering and ending fade. This is a modern civil-defence recording used for a fictional scene, not historical Singapore audio. The original synthetic `ww2-siren.wav` remains available but is unused.
